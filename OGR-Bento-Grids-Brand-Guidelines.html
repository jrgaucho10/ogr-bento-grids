01 · Brand story

Why OGR Bento Grids exists.

Bento grids are the default language of modern brand presentation — portfolios, case studies, social carousels, launch pages. Building them by hand is slow, rigid and repetitive. OGR Bento Grids makes the layout a one-click, live, editable system, so designers spend their time on content, not rectangles.

Mission

Make bento layouts a live, editable system instead of a stack of nudged rectangles — so consistency is the default, not a chore.

Vision

Every brand ships its case study, Behance post and Instagram carousel from one layout, in one click, with the brand already baked in.

Values

  • Power. More styles, more control, more output than anything else on the market.
  • Speed. Hours become seconds. Regenerate freely.
  • Control. Live while you tune, editable after you finalise.
  • Cohesion. One layout feeds every surface.

Positioning

For designers and studios who deliver brand identity work. OGR Bento Grids is the layout engine inside the OGR suite — the bridge between a finished logo and a finished presentation.

02 · Product

OGR Bento Grids.

A bento grid layout engine that lives inside Adobe Illustrator. Draw a rectangle, pick a style, tune with live sliders, shuffle until it fits, then finalise into plain editable shapes — and export to every format from one layout.

12+ layout styles

Simple, Centered, Brick, Random, Masonry, Asymmetric, Hero, Magazine, Dashboard, Social, Story, Collage — far beyond the basic four.

Live preview panel

A CEP panel previews the layout as you tune — no round-tripping to the artboard to see the result.

Auto masking

Drop images in and OGR fits, crops and masks them to each tile automatically — no manual clipping masks.

Brand-aware

Pulls colours and fonts from OGR Brand Guidelines / swatches, so every tile already wears the brand.

One-click export

PNG, JPG, PDF, SVG, WebP — plus platform presets for Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, Behance, A4 and slides.

Non-destructive

Keep the layout live and editable after generation — tune, shuffle and re-export without rebuilding.

Template library

Save, name and reuse layouts across projects. Share templates across the studio via Brand Hub.

AI-assisted layouts

Optional layout suggestions from your content — image aspect ratios, text length and brand rules inform the grid.

Batch variants

Generate N variations of the same settings in one run, then pick the winner.

04 · Logo

The mark.

A 2×2 grid. Two cells filled with the signal accent, two faded. It reads as a window, a grid, a system — and it is drawn from the same geometry the products generate. OGR Bento Grids inherits the OGR mark; the product name sits beside it as a secondary wordmark.

Always pair the mark with the OGR wordmark in first-use contexts. The product name "Bento Grids" follows in the lighter weight. The mark alone is reserved for app icons, favicons and tight spaces.

05 · Clearspace & minimum size

Give it room.

The exclusion zone is one quarter of the mark's height (X) on every side. Nothing — type, imagery, other logos — enters this zone.

X
OGR Bento Grids

Minimum print

24 mm wide for the horizontal lockup. Below this, use the mark only.

Minimum screen

96 px wide for the horizontal lockup. Below this, use the mark only.

Clearspace unit

X = 25% of the mark height. Applies to all four sides, always.

08 · Grid & spacing

The system behind the system.

An 8-point base grid. All spacing is a multiple of 8. Layouts use a 12-column grid with 24px gutters. The same grid the plugin generates — brand and product share one geometry.

Base unit

8 px. Every margin, padding and gap is a multiple of 8.

Columns

12 columns, 24 px gutters, 48 px outer margins on desktop.

Radius

4 / 8 / 12 / 16 px. Cards use 12, buttons 8, inputs 6.

Motion

150 ms ease-out for micro-interactions. Nothing bounces. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

09 · Iconography

Icons.

Stroke-based, 1.5 px, rounded caps and joins. 24×24 viewBox. Drawn to sit optically centred on the 8-point grid. Icons inherit text colour.

Icons inherit text colour. Use Ink on light, Paper on dark. Never fill icons with Signal — it competes with the mark.

13 · Do's & don'ts

Rules, not suggestions.

The mark is a system. Treat it like one.

Do

  • Use the mark and wordmark together in first-use contexts.
  • Keep the clearspace intact on every side.
  • Use Signal only for emphasis, CTAs and selection.
  • Scale uniformly — never stretch or squash.
  • Place on Paper or Ink backgrounds.
  • Inherit the OGR family identity across products.

Don't

  • Recolour the mark or the wordmark.
  • Rotate, skew or add effects (shadows, glows, outlines).
  • Crop the mark or remove a cell.
  • Place on busy photography or low-contrast backgrounds.
  • Use Signal as a large background fill.
  • Introduce new hues without updating this document.
14 · Accessibility

Everyone gets in.

Accessibility is a brand requirement, not a checklist. These are the minimums.

Contrast

Ink on Paper: 16.8:1. Signal on Ink: 11.2:1. Both pass AAA. Signal on Paper fails — never use for body text.

Motion

Respect prefers-reduced-motion. No auto-playing animation. Transitions under 200 ms.

Focus

Visible focus ring in Signal, 2 px, 2 px offset. Never remove outline without a replacement.
